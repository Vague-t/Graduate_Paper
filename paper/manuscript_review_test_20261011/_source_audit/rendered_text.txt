Abstract
End-to-end argument mining identifies text spans, argumentative roles, and directed relations. Component–relation
dependencies create cascading effects during both local paraphrasing and prediction repair. This thesis investigates
dependency maintenance in training and inference, building on multi-candidate reranking and bounded repair.
For training, relation influence domains identify internal, cross-boundary, and NONE factors. Lossless annotation transport
and factor masks define reliable correspondences for auxiliary supervision and paired distribution alignment, while original
documents retain full-graph generation targets. For inference, dependency-closed transactions jointly reconsider
component changes and all affected relations under frozen full-object scores, separate utility gates, and edit budgets. Local
and global optimization share the same object identity and constraints. CDCP collective-source relations are evaluated as
complete native hyperedges.
On the official AAEC and CDCP test splits, the complete configuration reaches strict relation F1 scores of 73.29 and 52.02,
improving over the inherited configuration by 8.10 and 13.26 points. The aggregate training and inference improvements
remain significant after Holm correction over fourteen primary comparisons. Paired-learning estimates are positive on both
corpora, but their separate contribution does not pass the family-level correction; joint acceptance and set actions likewise
lack confirmed independent advantages. A weak-scoring CDCP condition retains abstention as a failure analysis. Traceable
cases from four uploaded corpora illustrate both false-edge removal and true-edge loss, emphasizing that higher fixed
utility does not guarantee higher extraction F1.
Keywords: End-to-End Argument Mining; Reliable Correspondence; Paired Learning; Dependency Closure; Graph Repair;
Structured Prediction
