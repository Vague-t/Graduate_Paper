Abstract
In recent years, with the continuous advancement of Internet technology and intelligent
information services, Knowledge Graphs (KGs) have become an essential tool for knowledge
acquisition and representation in the field of Internet intelligence. Knowledge Graphs store
rich knowledge from the real world in the form of structured triplets, facilitating efficient
knowledge query and retrieval through graph algorithms on one hand; on the other hand, they
can serve as auxiliary information to support other knowledge-intensive tasks. However,
Knowledge Graphs are suffering the problem of information sparsity and incompleteness,
prompting researchers to focus on knowledge reasoning technologies to complete the missing
entities, relations and triplets within them. Current researches on knowledge reasoning tasks
typically employ neural network-based knowledge representation methods, which map
entities, relationships, and triplets of knowledge graphs into a low-dimensional vector space.
However, these methods mainly focus on the local structure of triplets but fail to fully explore
the global structural features, contextual knowledge, textual semantics, and temporal history
in Knowledge Graphs, making it difficult to achieve optimal knowledge reasoning
performance. Therefore, how to capture and integrate heterogeneous information in
Knowledge Graphs becomes an essential challenge for knowledge reasoning tasks, and it
contains the following two challenges: (1) How to effectively capture contextual information
in knowledge graphs and balance contextual information with global structural knowledge in
different levels; (2) Considering the dynamic characteristics of Knowledge Graphs, how to
capture temporal historical information and integrate it with textual semantics to perform
knowledge reasoning in various scenarios. To address the above challenges, this thesis mainly
contains the following two part of works:
